01Why this meeting

What forces would drive toward biology?

Much of today’s origins of life research has a synthetic chemistry bent: Which molecules came first, and how might they have been made prebiotically—RNA? Lipids? Biochemical networks before enzymes?

Our thematic meeting takes a different tack, following recent publications with a more biophysical focus. We will ask how evolutionary dynamics arose, how proteins got sequence–structure relationships, and what physical principles may have shaped the emergence of life.

03Importance & timeliness

A field ready to
connect the silos

There are arguably few questions in biophysics more important and less resolved than how life arose 3.5 billion years ago from simpler molecules.

The meeting comes at a moment of renewed activity: computer modeling of a new proteins-first view, work on the biophysical role of protein folding, and a growing base of experiments. We aim to build a community across astrobiology, synthetic chemistry, and biophysics—and across nucleic acids, membranes, and proteins.
